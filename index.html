Undergraduate Thesis · 2026

Investigation of Dielectric and Piezoelectric Properties of KNN with BCHfT Composite Ceramics

I developed lead-free piezoelectric ceramics as an environmentally friendly alternative to lead-based PZT, synthesizing K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (KNN) modified with Ba0.85Ca0.15Hf0.1Ti0.9O3 (BCHfT) by the conventional solid-state route. The composites were characterized by XRD, FE-SEM, and frequency- and temperature-dependent dielectric, impedance, and d33 measurements. KNN–1 wt% BCHfT gave the best balance of dielectric response and low loss, with the highest unpoled piezoelectric coefficient (15.5 pC/N) of the compositions studied.

Supervised by Prof. Dr. Ahmed Sharif↗ · Department of MME, BUET

Capstone Design Project · Team of 6

Millsorp — turning steel mill-scale waste into a heavy-metal water adsorbent

We converted ferrous mill scale, a low-value steel-industry waste, into a chemically treated, hematite-rich iron-oxide adsorbent and built a low-cost bench-scale filtration column to remove Cu, Cr and Ni from wastewater. With the iron-oxide phases confirmed by XRD and metal removal quantified by AAS and UV–Vis, the adsorbent reached up to ~95.8% removal in mixed-metal solutions — closing the loop from industrial waste to clean water and recoverable metal.

Failure analysis of a tensile-failed welded steel specimen

Specification Analysis · Individual

Reverse-engineering an unknown coated steel sheet

A multi-test identification of an unknown colour-coated sheet, layering evidence to reconstruct its full specification. Tensile testing, microstructure and Vickers hardness fingerprinted the base as a cold-rolled low-carbon steel; selective wet-chemical precipitation (NH₄OH → Al, (NH₄)₂HPO₄ → Zn) confirmed an Al–Zn metallic coating; and FTIR identified the organic polymer topcoat — together matching an ASTM A792M Grade 550 / AZM210 colour-coated roofing sheet.

MME 408 · Electrochemical Deposition

Immersion deposition of copper on steel

Displacement plating of copper onto a pickled mild-steel substrate from a CuSO₄ bath, driven by the galvanic couple in which iron dissolves anodically and Cu²⁺ is reduced onto the surface. I measured the deposit's weight, thickness and coverage per unit area, compared the practical deposit against the theoretical (density × volume) value, and traced the shortfall to insufficient bath time — plus why a complexed cyanide/sulphate bath with an external current gives a more adherent, controlled coating.

MME 408 · Combustion Analysis

Total carbon in steel by the Strohlein apparatus

Determined total carbon in a steel chip by combustion — oxidizing the sample under pure O₂ in a tube furnace, absorbing the evolved CO₂ in KOH, and reading the gas-volume change on the Strohlein burette. After a temperature- and pressure-based correction factor the result came to 0.321% C, a medium-carbon steel; the write-up covers the role of the tin flux and the H₂SO₄ oxygen dryer and places the composition on the Fe–C diagram.

MME 408 · Gravimetric Analysis

Determination of silicon in cast iron and steel

Gravimetric silicon determination: dissolving the steel in a stock acid mixture, dehydrating silicic acid to SiO₂, igniting the precipitate at 700 °C and weighing it to give 0.434% Si. MME 408 · Spectroscopic Analysis

Chemical composition of plain-carbon steel by OES

Measured the full elemental composition (wt%) of a plain-carbon steel by optical emission spectroscopy — sparking the polished sample at three spots, averaging, and applying CRM-based correction factors to the raw readings. The chemistry placed the steel close to AISI 1018; the discussion covers why industry favours OES's speed over XRF accuracy, why it struggles on grey cast iron, and the 100%-normalization quantification problem.

MME 408 · Hydrometallurgy

Study of leaching — ZnO in sulphuric acid

Acid leaching of ZnO in dilute H₂SO₄, recovering zinc as zinc pyrophosphate (Zn₂P₂O₇) by gravimetry and studying how temperature and stirring drive the reaction. Mapping the results onto the five kinetic steps of leaching showed recovery rising sharply with temperature under surface-reaction (Arrhenius) control with no stirring, while at room temperature agitation lifted recovery by thinning the diffusion boundary layer.

MME 408 · Mechanical Testing & Statistics

Weibull parameters from a three-point flexural test

Applied two-parameter Weibull statistics to the flexural strength of brittle ceramic (brick) specimens from a three-point bend test (ASTM C67-08), pooling every group's modulus-of-rupture data and extracting the Weibull modulus and normalizing stress by linear regression. I read the bimodal distribution as competing surface and internal flaws, and used the flexure-to-tensile conversion to estimate tensile strength where direct measurement isn't practical.

MME 408 · Nanomaterials Synthesis · Group

Preparation & characterization of hematite nanoparticles

Green synthesis of α-Fe₂O₃ (hematite) nanoparticles by a modified citrate sol-gel route from iron(III) nitrate and citric acid, annealed at 300–400 °C. The product was characterized by XRD (phase-pure hematite against JCPDS 86-0550, ~81% crystallinity, fully indexed peaks), UV-Vis with a Tauc plot for the optical band gap, and SEM for size and morphology — spherical particles, narrow distribution, mean near 185 nm.

MME 408 · Polymer Nanocomposites

PVA–hematite nanocomposite film

Fabricated a PVA–Fe₂O₃ nanocomposite film by solution casting — dissolving PVA, dispersing hematite nanoparticles, casting and drying — then probed the polymer–nanoparticle interaction by FTIR. Comparing the composite against pristine PVA showed how the O–H, C=O and C–O bands shift and broaden through hydrogen bonding and Fe–O coordination, and the report sets out practical routes to lower PVA's water solubility (crosslinking, hydrophobic grafting, nanoparticle barriers).
